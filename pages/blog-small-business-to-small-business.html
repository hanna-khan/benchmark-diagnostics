Small Business to Small Business

Supporting independent healthcare partnerships

Key takeaways

  • Support for small business is well documented in B2C, but far less attention goes to B2B among small enterprises.
  • Independent labs and practices face falling reimbursements, compliance burden and costly electronic systems.
  • SB-to-SB collaboration preserves local expertise, responsiveness and accountability.

01Small business support, beyond consumers

According to surveys commissioned by organizations such as the Public Affairs Council, most Americans express strong support for small businesses, and many say they are willing to pay more to support them.

While this sentiment is well documented in business-to-consumer (B2C) settings, far less attention has been given to business-to-business (B2B) relationships among small enterprises.

02A difficult economic environment

Unlike large corporations with extensive capital reserves and negotiating power, independent businesses must balance profitability with survival. Rising operational costs, inflation, regulatory burdens, and technology mandates place disproportionate pressure on smaller organizations.

Healthcare is a prime example. Independent laboratories and physician practices face declining reimbursements, increasing compliance requirements, and the costly transition to ever-expanding electronic systems. Businesses that fail to keep pace may face financial penalties, workflow inefficiencies, or loss of competitiveness.

03Scale versus service

Large national corporations often compete by leveraging scale, bundled services, sophisticated electronic integrations, and aggressive contracting strategies. The quality of service can be overshadowed by operational convenience and corporate purchasing power rather than by diagnostic excellence, accessibility, and personalized support.

Faster communication, greater accountability, and closer physician collaboration.

Independent laboratories often provide exactly that, yet find it increasingly difficult to compete. Many small physician groups likewise feel compelled to consolidate simply to improve negotiating leverage with insurance carriers and vendors.

Regulators have attempted to address certain anti-competitive and inducement-related practices, including inappropriate financial incentives tied to referrals. However, smaller businesses frequently lack the legal and financial resources that large corporate entities have to navigate complex regulatory environments.

04Strengthening alliances

Rather than waiting for policy changes alone, small businesses should consider strengthening alliances with one another. Small Business-to-Small Business (SB-to-SB) collaboration can help preserve local expertise, improve responsiveness, support independent professionals, and strengthen regional economies.

Supporting small businesses is not merely a financial decision; it is an investment in community relationships, accountability, and long-term sustainability.
